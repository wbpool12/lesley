Understanding the Real Estate TREC 1-4 Family Residential Contract

February 26, 2025

The TREC 1-4 Family Residential Contract is the standard document used in most residential real estate transactions in Texas. Whether you are a first-time buyer or a seasoned investor, understanding the key sections of this contract is essential to protecting your interests. One area that often causes confusion is Section 6A, which deals with title policy and boundary coverage.

Section 6A: Title Policy & Boundary Coverage

Section 6A of the TREC contract addresses the type of title insurance policy the buyer will receive at closing. Title insurance protects the buyer against defects in the title, such as liens, errors in public records, or ownership disputes. Within this section, buyers are presented with two options regarding boundary coverage, each offering a different level of protection.

Option 1: Minimal Protection

Under the first option, the buyer receives a standard title insurance policy. While this policy covers many common title defects, it does NOT cover boundary disputes, encroachments, or shortages in acreage. This means that if a neighbor's fence extends onto your property, or if a survey reveals that your lot is smaller than expected, the standard policy will not provide coverage for those issues. This option typically comes with a lower premium but leaves the buyer exposed to potential boundary-related problems.

Option 2: Broader Coverage

The second option provides an enhanced title policy that DOES cover boundary disputes, encroachments, and shortages in area. This broader coverage offers significantly more protection, ensuring that if survey-related issues arise after closing, the buyer has recourse through their title insurance. The premium for this option is slightly higher, but the additional peace of mind is well worth the investment for most buyers.

Which Option Should You Choose?

For most buyers, Option 2 is the recommended choice. Boundary disputes and encroachment issues can be costly and stressful to resolve, and the small difference in premium is a worthwhile investment to protect against these risks. However, every situation is unique. It is always best to consult with your real estate agent and title company to determine which option is right for your specific transaction.
