Understanding the Real Estate TREC 1-4 Family Residential Contract

February 26, 2025

The TREC (Texas Real Estate Commission) 1-4 Family Residential Contract is the standard document used in virtually all residential real estate transactions across Texas. Whether you are buying or selling a home, understanding this contract is essential to protecting your interests and ensuring a smooth closing process. One area that often raises questions among buyers and sellers is Section 6A, which deals with boundary-related title policy options.

Section 6A: Title Policy & Boundary Coverage

Section 6A of the TREC 1-4 contract addresses the type of title insurance policy the buyer will receive and, importantly, how boundary-related issues are handled. Buyers are presented with two options, each offering a different level of protection.

Option 1: Minimal Protection

Under Option 1, the buyer receives a standard title policy that does not include coverage for boundary disputes, encroachments, or shortages in area. This means that if a neighbor's fence, driveway, or structure crosses onto your property line — or if the actual lot size differs from what was represented — you would not be covered by the title policy. Option 1 is the more basic and less expensive choice, but it leaves the buyer exposed to potentially costly boundary-related issues that may not surface until well after closing.

Option 2: Broader Coverage

Option 2 provides a more comprehensive title policy that does cover boundary disputes, encroachments, and shortages in area. With this option, if a survey reveals that a neighboring structure encroaches onto your property, or if a boundary dispute arises after closing, the title insurance company would step in to help resolve the matter and cover associated costs. While this option typically comes with a slightly higher premium, it offers significantly greater peace of mind — especially for properties where boundary lines may be ambiguous, fences have been moved over the years, or the property has not been recently surveyed.

Which Option Should You Choose?

For most buyers, Option 2 is the recommended choice. Boundary issues are more common than many people realize, and the additional cost of enhanced coverage is minimal compared to the potential expense of resolving a boundary dispute out of pocket. However, every situation is different, and the right choice depends on the specific property, its history, and your individual risk tolerance.

As with any aspect of a real estate contract, it is always wise to consult with your real estate agent or title company before making a decision. An experienced professional can walk you through the details of each option, explain how they apply to your specific transaction, and help you make an informed choice that protects your investment for years to come.
