The Christmas Capital of Texas

Grapevine, Texas

Welcome to Grapevine

Nestled between Dallas and Fort Worth and just minutes from DFW International Airport, Grapevine is a city of roughly 54,000 residents that effortlessly blends small-town heritage with big-city amenities. Its historic downtown along Main Street is the heartbeat of the community, lined with locally owned boutiques, tasting rooms, and restaurants housed in beautifully preserved 19th-century buildings.

Grapevine proudly carries the title "Christmas Capital of Texas," hosting more than 1,400 holiday events each season -- from the iconic North Pole Express on the Grapevine Vintage Railroad to the dazzling ICE! exhibition at the Gaylord Texan Resort & Convention Center. But the festivity extends well beyond December: GrapeFest, the largest wine festival in the Southwest, draws visitors every September, and the Urban Wine Trail connects more than a dozen wineries and tasting rooms within walking distance of one another.

Outdoor enthusiasts are drawn to Grapevine Lake, a 7,380-acre U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reservoir offering boating, kayaking, fishing, swimming beaches, and over 20 miles of shoreline trails. For shopping, Grapevine Mills is one of the largest outlet and value-retail destinations in North Texas, featuring more than 180 stores, a SEA LIFE Aquarium, and LEGOLAND Discovery Center.

Families benefit from the highly regarded Grapevine-Colleyville Independent School District (GCISD), which consistently earns top marks for academics, athletics, and fine arts. With a walkable downtown, a thriving arts and culture scene, and a location that puts the entire metroplex within easy reach, Grapevine delivers an exceptional quality of life for residents of all ages.

Location of Grapevine, TX

Grapevine occupies a prime position in the heart of the Dallas-Fort Worth metroplex in Tarrant County. Bordered by Southlake to the west, Colleyville to the south, and Grapevine Lake to the north, the city sits directly adjacent to DFW International Airport -- one of the busiest airports in the world. This strategic location gives residents easy access to Highways 114, 121, and 360, placing downtown Dallas approximately 25 miles to the east and downtown Fort Worth about 25 miles to the southwest.

Living in Grapevine: Pros & Cons

5 Reasons You'll Love It

  • Historic Downtown Charm

    Grapevine's Main Street is one of the most charming historic districts in North Texas. Stroll past restored brick storefronts, browse independent shops, and enjoy farm-to-table dining in a walkable, pedestrian-friendly setting that feels worlds away from typical suburban sprawl.

  • Wine Culture & Urban Wine Trail

    With more than a dozen wineries and tasting rooms clustered along its Urban Wine Trail, Grapevine has established itself as the wine capital of Texas. From bold Tempranillos to crisp Texas whites, you can spend an entire afternoon sampling local vintages without ever getting in a car.

  • Grapevine Lake Recreation

    Grapevine Lake offers 7,380 acres of water and over 60 miles of shoreline for boating, fishing, paddleboarding, and swimming. The surrounding parks feature scenic hiking and mountain biking trails, picnic areas, and campgrounds that keep outdoor lovers busy year-round.

  • Holiday Festivals & Events

    As the official Christmas Capital of Texas, Grapevine hosts over 1,400 events during the holiday season, including the North Pole Express, Parade of Lights, and ICE! at the Gaylord Texan. Beyond the holidays, GrapeFest, Butterflies in the Garden, and Main Street Days keep the calendar packed with family-friendly celebrations.

  • Proximity to DFW Airport

    Grapevine sits directly adjacent to DFW International Airport, making it one of the most convenient locations in the metroplex for frequent travelers. Most residents can reach the terminal in under 10 minutes, a perk that business professionals and globetrotters highly value.

5 Reasons You May Not

  • Airport Noise

    Living near one of the busiest airports in the world comes with trade-offs. Some neighborhoods, particularly those closest to DFW Airport's flight paths, experience regular aircraft noise that can be noticeable, especially during early morning and late evening hours.

  • Tourist Crowds (Especially Holidays)

    Grapevine's popularity as a tourist destination means that Main Street, the Gaylord Texan, and Grapevine Mills can become extremely crowded during the holiday season, GrapeFest, and summer weekends. Locals sometimes find it challenging to navigate their own downtown during peak events.

  • Traffic Congestion

    The convergence of Highways 114, 121, and 360 near the airport creates significant traffic bottlenecks, particularly during rush hours. Event weekends and holiday tourism compound the congestion, making commute times unpredictable.

  • Summer Heat & Humidity

    Like much of North Texas, Grapevine endures hot, humid summers with temperatures regularly exceeding 100 degrees Fahrenheit from June through August. The proximity to the lake can add extra humidity, making outdoor activities less comfortable during peak summer months.

  • Higher Property Taxes

    Texas has no state income tax, but Tarrant County property tax rates tend to be above the national average. In Grapevine, homeowners should expect a total effective tax rate that reflects the city's quality schools, infrastructure, and services -- a consideration for budget-conscious buyers.

Home Styles In Grapevine, TX

Grapevine's housing stock reflects the city's rich heritage and evolving tastes. From lovingly restored historic homes near downtown to newer custom builds in master-planned communities, the architectural diversity is one of the things that makes Grapevine's real estate market so appealing. Here are three of the most popular styles you will find throughout the city.

Victorian

Grapevine's historic core features charming Victorian-era homes that date back to the city's founding in the mid-1800s. These residences are distinguished by ornate woodwork, wraparound porches, steeply pitched rooflines, and decorative trim. Many have been lovingly restored while retaining their original character, and they sit on tree-shaded lots within walking distance of Main Street. Living in a Grapevine Victorian means owning a piece of Texas history while enjoying the convenience of a modern metroplex location.

Craftsman

The Craftsman style is well-represented in Grapevine's established neighborhoods, offering a warm, handcrafted aesthetic that emphasizes natural materials and thoughtful detail. Expect tapered columns on stone piers, exposed rafter tails, wide front porches, and open interior floor plans with built-in cabinetry. These homes typically range from 1,800 to 3,500 square feet and appeal to buyers who appreciate artisanal craftsmanship, durable construction, and a cozy sense of place.

Mediterranean

Found primarily in Grapevine's newer subdivisions and custom-lot communities, Mediterranean-inspired homes bring a resort-like ambiance to the North Texas landscape. Stucco exteriors in warm earth tones, clay tile roofs, arched entryways, wrought-iron balconies, and interior courtyards define this style. These properties often feature open-concept living areas, gourmet kitchens, and outdoor living spaces designed for entertaining -- perfect for homeowners who want a touch of Old World elegance with modern functionality.

Schools in Grapevine, TX

Grapevine is served by the Grapevine-Colleyville Independent School District (GCISD), one of the most respected school districts in the state of Texas. GCISD serves approximately 14,000 students across 22 campuses and is known for strong academic performance, competitive athletic programs, award-winning fine arts, and a commitment to career and technical education.

Elementary schools such as Grapevine Elementary, Silver Lake Elementary, and Timberline Elementary consistently receive high marks from parents and the Texas Education Agency. At the secondary level, Grapevine Middle School and Cross Timbers Middle School prepare students for the rigorous curriculum at Grapevine High School, which offers a wide range of Advanced Placement courses, dual-credit college programs, and specialized academies.

Grapevine High School's Mustangs have a storied tradition in athletics, particularly in football, baseball, and track and field. The district's fine arts programs are equally impressive, with award-winning band, choir, orchestra, theater, and visual arts programs that regularly compete at the state level. Private and parochial school options are also available in the surrounding area for families seeking alternatives.

Places of Interest in Grapevine

The heart and soul of Grapevine, Historic Downtown spans several blocks along Main Street and features more than 80 locally owned shops, restaurants, art galleries, and wineries. The district hosts seasonal festivals, live music, and community events throughout the year. Beautifully preserved 19th-century architecture and a pedestrian-friendly streetscape make it one of the most walkable and enjoyable downtown areas in the DFW metroplex.

Learn more about Historic Downtown Grapevine

The Grapevine Vintage Railroad operates authentic 1920s-era coaches along a historic cotton belt route between Grapevine and the Fort Worth Stockyards. During the holiday season, the railroad transforms into the North Pole Express, one of the most popular family experiences in North Texas, complete with holiday characters, storytelling, and hot cocoa. Year-round excursions offer a nostalgic glimpse into the golden age of rail travel.

Learn more about the Grapevine Vintage Railroad

Managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, Grapevine Lake covers 7,380 acres and offers a full spectrum of outdoor recreation. Popular activities include boating, sailing, fishing for largemouth bass and catfish, paddleboarding, and swimming at designated beach areas. The surrounding parks -- including Meadowmere Park, Oak Grove Park, and Murrell Park -- provide hiking trails, picnic facilities, campgrounds, and scenic overlooks.

Learn more about Grapevine Lake

The Gaylord Texan is a world-class resort and convention center located on the shores of Grapevine Lake. Its stunning glass-enclosed atrium features lush gardens, waterways, and Texas-themed landscapes. The resort hosts major events year-round, including the legendary ICE! holiday exhibition, SummerFest, and the Texan Fitness Trail. With multiple restaurants, a full-service spa, water park, and extensive meeting facilities, the Gaylord Texan is both a top employer and a major economic anchor for the city.

Learn more about the Gaylord Texan Resort

Grapevine Mills is one of the largest outlet and value-retail destinations in North Texas, featuring more than 180 stores including Last Call by Neiman Marcus, Nike Factory Store, and H&M. Beyond shopping, the complex houses LEGOLAND Discovery Center, SEA LIFE Grapevine Aquarium, AMC Theatres, and Peppa Pig World of Play -- making it a full-day destination for families. The mall draws visitors from across the region and contributes significantly to Grapevine's robust retail economy.
